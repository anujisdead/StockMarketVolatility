Our Analytical Approach

1. Data Acquisition

We fetch daily closing prices from Yahoo Finance API (`yfinance`) for 80 major Indian stocks (BSE SENSEX 30 + NSE NIFTY 50). The dataset spans a rolling 10-year window to ensure robustness against various market conditions.

Log Returns Calculation:
r_t = ln(P_t / P_{t-1})

2. Mean Equation (ARMA)

We model the returns series using an Auto-Regressive Moving Average (ARMA) model. Based on our initial research (Goal 1), an ARMA(2,3) specification was found to best minimize the AIC for the reference index (SENSEX).

  • AR(2): Depends on returns of the last 2 days.
  • MA(3): Depends on forecast errors of the last 3 days.

3. Volatility Equation (GARCH/TGARCH)

Financial time series exhibit "volatility clustering"—periods of high risk follow high risk. To capture this, we apply a Generalized Auto-Regressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity (GARCH) model to the residuals of the ARMA model.

The Leverage Effect (TGARCH)

Standard GARCH assumes symmetric response to news. However, markets panic more on bad news. We use Threshold GARCH (TGARCH / GJR-GARCH) to capture this asymmetry.

If the leverage term (Gamma) is positive and significant, it indicates that negative returns increase future volatility more than positive returns of the same magnitude.

4. Signal Generation

Our investment signals are derived from the forecasted 5-day volatility ($\sigma_{t+5}$):

BUY Volatility is falling (>5% drop) or remains extremely low.

SELL Volatility is spiking (>5% rise) indicating potential instability.

HOLD Volatility is stable.

CAUTION High 'Gamma' detected—stock is highly sensitive to bad news.
